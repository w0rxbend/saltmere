**Gist.** The `ENS160+AHT21` combination breakout puts two inter-integrated circuit (I2C) parts on one bus: ScioSense's ENS160, a metal-oxide (MOX) gas sensor reporting an air-quality index (AQI), total volatile organic compounds (TVOC) and equivalent carbon dioxide (eCO2); and Aosong's AHT21 temperature and relative-humidity (RH) sensor. **The eCO2 channel is computed from the VOC signal, not from a carbon-dioxide measurement**, so it responds to any VOC source in the room. The cost of the arrangement is interpretive: the numbers are only meaningful after a warm-up interval, and only after external temperature and humidity have been written back into the gas sensor.

## What the eCO2 channel is

A non-dispersive infrared (NDIR) carbon-dioxide sensor passes infrared light through a gas chamber and measures the absorption attributable to CO2. The ENS160 contains no such optical path. It heats a metal-oxide film and observes how the film's resistance changes as volatile organic compounds adsorb on its surface. **The eCO2 output is derived from that VOC signal**, which is why the datasheet labels it *equivalent* rather than measured CO2.

The consequence is a class of readings that are correct as VOC measurements and wrong as CO2 measurements. **A solvent source near the sensor — a marker pen, hand sanitiser, cleaning product — raises eCO2 without any corresponding change in carbon-dioxide concentration.** The channel functions as a proxy for a stuffy or contaminated room; it does not function as a ventilation-compliance instrument. TVOC, in parts per billion (ppb), is the reading closest to the physical quantity the hardware transduces. Where a true CO2 concentration is required, an NDIR part such as the SCD41 is the appropriate sensor.

## Addressing

The two chips occupy distinct I2C addresses and share one bus without arbitration:

- **AHT21 is fixed at `0x38`.**
- **ENS160 is `0x52` with its ADDR pin low and `0x53` with it high.** The combo boards tie the pin high, so a bus scan reports `0x53`.

## Operating modes and warm-up

The ENS160's `OPMODE` register at `0x10` selects the device state: `0x00` deep sleep, `0x01` idle, `0x02` standard mode with active gas sensing, `0xF0` reset. Continuous measurement requires standard mode.

Two datasheet timings determine when output is usable. **A warm-up of roughly three minutes follows power-on before readings settle**, and **a fresh sensor requires up to one hour of initial conditioning on its first run**. Data read during these intervals is not representative of the ambient gas concentration. The device exposes its own judgement through the `DEVICE_STATUS` register at `0x20`, which carries two separate bit fields. **`NEWDAT` says a fresh sample is available; the two-bit `VALIDITY FLAG` says which phase the sensor is in** — normal operation, warm-up, initial start-up, or invalid output. The validity flag, not a host-side timer, is the correct gate for deciding that a sample means anything.

## Compensation inputs

MOX film resistance varies with temperature and humidity independently of gas concentration, so the ENS160 accepts externally supplied ambient values and applies them to its computation. This is the role the AHT21 fills on the shared board. Two registers carry the values, both in fixed-point encodings:

- **`TEMP_IN` (`0x13`): temperature in kelvin multiplied by 64.**
- **`RH_IN` (`0x15`): relative humidity in percent multiplied by 512.**

The SparkFun ENS160 library accepts degrees Celsius and percent and performs the scaling, so the packing need not be done by hand. Fresh values are written on each measurement cycle; stale compensation degrades the gas output in exactly the conditions where it matters most, namely a room whose temperature or humidity is changing.

## Reading the three outputs on an ESP32

Two libraries cover the parts: **SparkFun Indoor Air Quality Sensor - ENS160** (`SparkFun_ENS160`) and **Adafruit AHTX0**, which supports the AHT20 and AHT21.

```cpp
#include <Wire.h>
#include <SparkFun_ENS160.h>   // "SparkFun Indoor Air Quality Sensor - ENS160"
#include <Adafruit_AHTX0.h>    // "Adafruit AHTX0"

SparkFun_ENS160 ens160;
Adafruit_AHTX0  aht;

void setup() {
  Serial.begin(115200);
  Wire.begin(21, 22);          // ESP32 default SDA=21, SCL=22

  if (!aht.begin())            { Serial.println("AHT21 not found");  while (1); }
  if (!ens160.begin(0x53))     { Serial.println("ENS160 not found"); while (1); }

  ens160.setOperatingMode(SFE_ENS160_RESET);
  delay(100);
  ens160.setOperatingMode(SFE_ENS160_STANDARD);
}

void loop() {
  sensors_event_t humidity, temp;
  aht.getEvent(&humidity, &temp);

  // Compensation must reach the ENS160 before the gas registers are read
  ens160.setTempCompensationCelsius(temp.temperature);
  ens160.setRHCompensationFloat(humidity.relative_humidity);

  // checkDataStatus() is NEWDAT only; getFlags() == 0 is the "normal operation"
  // validity state, so both are needed to gate on a settled sample
  if (ens160.checkDataStatus() && ens160.getFlags() == 0) {
    Serial.printf("T=%.1fC RH=%.1f%%  AQI=%d  TVOC=%dppb  eCO2=%dppm\n",
                  temp.temperature,
                  humidity.relative_humidity,
                  ens160.getAQI(),          // 1..5 (UBA scale)
                  ens160.getTVOC(),         // ppb
                  ens160.getECO2());        // ppm-equivalent, VOC-derived
  }
  delay(1000);
}
```

`checkDataStatus()` alone reports only that a new sample arrived — it is true during warm-up as well — so the validity flag must be tested beside it for the loop to stay silent until the sensor reports normal operation. **The AQI returned is the 1–5 index on the UBA scale (1 excellent, 5 unhealthy), not the 0–500 United States Environmental Protection Agency AQI** — a second label that resembles a familiar quantity while denoting a different one.

Dashboard labelling follows from the above: TVOC in ppb, and eCO2 annotated as VOC-derived rather than presented alongside measured concentrations without qualification.

**Try next.** Log TVOC and eCO2 to InfluxDB or MQTT for a day, then introduce a marker pen or open a window near the sensor. The eCO2 trace moving with the solvent source demonstrates the derivation from VOCs directly.

## Pitfalls

- **A bus scan finds nothing at `0x52`.** The combo boards tie ADDR high; the ENS160 answers at `0x53`, and `begin()` called with the default address fails.
- **eCO2 spikes with no occupancy change.** A VOC source — solvent, cleaning product, alcohol gel — has raised the MOX signal from which eCO2 is computed; carbon-dioxide concentration is unaffected.
- **Readings during the first minutes look plausible but drift.** Warm-up takes about three minutes after power-on, and a never-before-run sensor needs up to an hour of conditioning; gating on a host-side delay, or on `NEWDAT` alone, instead of the `DEVICE_STATUS` validity flag publishes this transient.
- **Gas readings shift when the room heats up or a humidifier runs.** MOX resistance depends on temperature and humidity; compensation values written once at startup and never refreshed leave the ENS160 correcting against stale ambient conditions.
- **The AQI value is treated as an EPA index.** The ENS160 reports the 1–5 UBA scale, so a value of 5 is a category, not a point on the 0–500 scale.
- **Raw register writes produce nonsense compensation.** `TEMP_IN` is kelvin × 64 and `RH_IN` is percent × 512; passing degrees Celsius or plain percent to those registers writes a value orders of magnitude off.
